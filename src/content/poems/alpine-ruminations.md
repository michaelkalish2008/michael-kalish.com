# **Note from the author** {#note-from-the-author}

This book of poems, Alpine Ruminations, is an account of my 20s, during which time I took countless jobs across the fields of education and agriculture, including work as a commercial beekeeper, enologist assistant, herdsman, shepherd, cheesemaker and butcher (among many others) across France, Switzerland, Italy and the United States.

The book of poems is an effort to express the cold moments of pain and beauty that I experienced while working alongside farmers, migrants, shop keepers, butchers, cheesemakers, people of various trades and the animals we worked with. 

When I look back, the hardest part of the journey was witnessing the treatment of certain people and animals caught in the gears of both traditional and modern agriculture. I hope the writing helps you and others find compassion and the courage to make a new start.


# **Awake** {#awake}

Basins shine plates of fresh cream,

And rising through the smooth folds of sleep,

I drift under the still, embroidered bells,

While morning chores ring in the barn’s bovine clatter,

And braids of light fray outside the chalet door,

Sweeping over the ears of grass,

Sparking green and wild.



# **Building** {#building}

With posts and steel javelin over my shoulder,

I bore holes along the ridge,

And plant each post neatly with my mallet,

Contact sends fraught notes to the valley,

The dry fence begs for wire like bread,

While the mountain whistles its northerly tune,

And builds without a nail.


# **Loneliness** {#loneliness}

Where is the blacksmith and his shy son,

Or the chatty carpenter and tall lumberjack?

The chalet door is swept and clean,

And welcomes strangers with still gestures,

I stand at my stump and chop wood,

That claps the ground with each kiss of the hatchet,

And resigned to chores, I consign my thoughts,

To cats stretching on golden columns of hay,

And the warm consolation of purring beams.



# **Wisdom** {#wisdom}

I am earnest and eager,

A problem solver and conversationalist,

How then does wisdom’s trail elude me?

Above every climb is a new one,

Why does wisdom sprawl endlessly like grass?

Has it never come crashing down like a rock,

And echoed for the valley to hear?



# **Promise** {#promise}

My mind like an udder swollen and hot,

Waits for the clang of the milk can,

So I can pull from my tether,

And gallop through the chalet door,

But on a stool, thumb and finger cinched,

Thought like milk pings and shushes the wall of my silver can,

trembling and expanding my hope splashes shapeless,

But believe in me,

Watch my knitting grains,

And I promise you,

I will gell.



# **Rumors** {#rumors}

Up, in the mountains,

The winds run the puppet show,

Sharp and dry, it swings the clothesline,

The socks and chemises undulate in snapping melody,

The towels lift in an eddy of play,

The trousers and buttons clap and jingle,

Wind stirs the pointed tips of slope and field,

the smooth melt of snow and needles of pines,

Stirring the gases steaming from the compost,

And the flies beading in the eyes of cattle.

It is here, now gone, like brush strokes,

On the back of chamois darting over the ridge,

Over the unyielding articulations of rock,

And wormed carcasses discovered dozens of times with the same silent exclamations,

Stroking bronze arms cooled by work’s wet wage,

Down, it goes, over pools of mud,

Over the small town talk of rain,

Borrowing the faces of all that it can touch.

Down, the wind twists and appears,

Through the leaves and gold medallions,

And thyme and raspberries,

Puffing across the lake,

Rippling my young and hopeful glances.



# **Breathe** {#breathe}

In my cot above the barn,

The walls made of Swiss timber are still,

From the rafters bells hang latched,

My clothes are cleanly stacked above their drawers,

And there above my bed is my goat skin to be made into a bag,

By the faces I have yet to see and hands yet to shake.

Clouds blow in from the North,

The barn doors are closed and the aisles litter with chafe and dung,

The Simmental and Brown Swiss huff over straw,

Their backs hulking, warm and undulating,

In the loft above I am taken by the undertow of sleep,

Over waves of bovine respiration,

To a rice field of stars.


# **Time** {#time}

Thunder rolls over the mountain,

Awake I descend the creaking staircase

And cross by the copper vat,

Pass the wood chopping block,

The white wedded cheese in its veiled press,

The boiler and draining table,

Pass the pool of milk and rising cream,

And goat tommes,

Pass the kitchen and milk pump,

Out the door into the sharp night air,

Where lightning snatches night’s velvet gown,

After the long blink of sleep, the sun rises askance,

The cows pull on their tethers and their tails sweep the barn light,

I see the last millennial squeeze of milk and the return of more,

Up they go, into the mountains, through time’s chalet door.



# **Moving on** {#moving-on}

The Berger’s coarse, firm grip paid farewell,

My pack stuffed with clothes and goat skin,

My heaviest boots on my feet to lighten my load,

The mountains are behind me,

The road opens wide and consumes me.

Driving down the slope in the my friend’s car,

The wind strokes my hair and chin,

My friend is smiling and pointing from the car to where the climbers fell and died,

I feel for my envelope of francs,

Where does one go after these mountains?

Do I go back to the barns,

Where the strong and hooved are standing and suckling,

And my hope sinks with the weak into the straw-filled corners?

Where the pace of the living is matched by the push of my wheelbarrow,

Of limp runts and poor miscarriages,

Dumped into the compost to ferment in peace.

Do I go back to the fields,

Where the herd swings and stalls,

Cycling through episodes of panic and complacency,

Through theater of appetite and stages of flowers,

Through barnyard dressing rooms and rehearsal,

Chewing on the regurgitations of grass and straw?

Do I wander back to the farm after the cows are walked to their lot,

To browse the hills, meadows and fences,

To admire the slow and giant growth of paddocks,

Only to go down to my garden to dig and pick,

Alternating hands with tools not my own,

To throw my strength like a murderer onto the ground?



# **Slaughter** {#slaughter}

I join the whistling butcher to the slaughterhouse,

To lead the red painted young from the truck,

To drop the heavy pistol on the crown.

Tick. And it is all over.

The knife slicing the carotid and jugular,

And like billiards balls life breaks and sinks into the pockets of the kill box.

The hind leg is shackled and the carcass is hoisted,

The pigs take their bath and shave and the ruminants are removed from their rugs,

the carcasses are split like a sentence,

And all of their vowels eviscerated are hung for inspection,

The intestines plunk into buckets and the stomachs are emptied with a knife,

Green and fibrous dung sinks into receptacles.

The now sleepy butcher looks at his watch,

The stomachs are cooked and combed with a spoon,

Vapor floats off the tripe table and tangles as its rises,

The carcasses are pushed on the track overhead,

The butcher climbs his step ladder,

Off come the hooves.

The electric saw spins and the ruminant carcasses butterfly neatly,

The scimitar taps its mark and falls deftly on the tail bone of the hog,

Down, it clacks the vertebrae,

Tending first right then left,

The butcher furrows his brow and buries his tongue in his cheek,

Crack goes the thoracic, the cervical and the head.



# **Lunch** {#lunch}

Into the cold room, the trim and symmetrical carcasses swing and touch,

The fridge door clunks shut and the butcher checks his watch,

Upstairs, the table is set and I can hear the butcher’s wife call my name,

The hot stairwell is full of clambering feet,

The ice in tall glasses stir and clink,

And the butcher’s son shows me my seat and asks kindly how I am.

I have climbed bales to the ceiling,

And have fallen asleep in barns purring with cats,

I have clapped squares of clover and alfalfa to ribbons,

But nothing shakes those frantic looks of the searching mothers,

Who swell and mourn with the bounty of their milk,

Bleating and lowing for a tongue that cannot answer.

At the slaughterhouse the trucks are still,

The pale walls are splashed with water and light,

The floors are brushed and washed down.

Outside the facility is the dumpster stained and crusted.

The butcher washes his arms up to the elbow,

And sails across the kitchen on the long keel of joy,

And lifting his glass, it’s a toast to health,

The meal commences and the tune of death carries into another room,

Bone fragments and cartilage bury in soup.



# **Grapes** {#grapes}

Below the canopies I twist and gnarl with my stock shallow and new,

Half my bunches are raisins good for dropping on the ground,

But take a grape or two and punch my dusty skins,

Point the refract-o-meter to the light,

I am an acre ripening in the dust.


# **Wine** {#wine}

Into the slatted crates I fall over and over,

The hooded pickers shuttle their buckets in the dark,

Their hands are stained and cold,

Far from the field into the hopper I go,

Trampled by the curl of the steel augur.

My pulp and skins flood the fermentation tanks,

I circulate the hoses, gaskets, clamps and pump,

Gases bubble to my throat,

My ruby thoughts return, my apple voice returns,

My heart is racked and pumping

hooped and tumbling in its toasted barrel.

And pressing against the staves I calm and settle,

There is a clink and footsteps by the cellar door,

Up, a young man climbs the columns,

pulling the corks from their bungholes one by one,

And giving a stir with steel headed revolutions,

With yeast and sediment up flies my velvet robe.

Racked from the lees of purple equilibrium,

the familiar staves and hoops of home,

the wooden heads and chimes of my arrogance,

Into the sleepy interludes of dry and expanding oak,

To the resounding clink of time’s cellar door.

And into the bottle I go,

Rolling down over the bars, bolts, and washers into glass,

Measured in milliliters and corked,

The assembly line clacks and jingles,

I sit red and upright, cased and labeled,

My brand and vintage front and center.

I pass from hand to hand,

A gift of guesswork, time, and budget,

And out I pour at the beginning of the meal,

Raised in a handful of glass.



# **Butcher shop** {#butcher-shop}

In the butcher shop, the lamb casings are plump and coiled,

The salami hang dusted on loops of string,

Women mill about the register and deli cases,

Ting, there is a sideways shuffle and animation,

I am next and the butcher gives me a salutary wink,

In come the pork chops, pâté, and rillettes.

The customer ahead reads from her list,

The butcher’s wife folds and tapes the cuts in parchment,

And shuffles mid-sentence to the register,

The butcher pushes the slicer like a joyful oarsmen,

And balances his keel with a hearty laugh,

Then orbiting the store, the butcher shares samples of his rillette,

His apron clean and tied firmly at the back,

The town and his sons are on his tongue,

The world in a basket is on his tongue,

The knife is tabled for a handshake,

His sample for the jingle of bells.



# **Bee yard** {#bee-yard}

Across the field with gloves and mask,

My boss waves his smoker,

Flashing with dry grass and burlap.

And with the faith of pallets and nails,

Head lowered I crossed the yard,

Through the parting pixelations of a humming sea.

The dying are dragged from the wood of the living,

The dull noises of death vibrate in the grass,

The lean frames are looted,

The field mouse burrows its way in,

Along with lizards and black widows,

And the dry flood of ants

The cold navy blanket falls over the yard,

The semi is loaded and ready,

I am raised on the forklift to the top of the third hive,

And jump and run along the wooden roofs of millions,

Two hundred and eighty two hives are squared and tightened,

I cross the highway with my truck and trailer,

And set my speed and course,

In the dark, headlights expand and vanish,

Brake lights glow and fade,

And for a moment I can sneak between the frames,

And drink from the comb of the open road.



# **Almond Orchard** {#almond-orchard}

In the orchard it is midnight,

The straps are loosened and lobbed to the ground,

The hives are forklifted down the lanes,

Up, the East spins yarn that shuttles through the branches,

On to the road my friend and I go, a couple of dusty boots,

Stubble and jackets, joined together like spit in the dirt.

Back in the almonds, pink blossoms twitch and hum,

I fill the feeders of four hives, and clack,

Syrup follows me back to the truck bed,

I recline as we motor to the next,

My pry tool on my chest, my hands behind my head,

The velvet gloves of sleep running up my sleeves.

The summit of day is behind us,

The trailer is hitched,

The truck keys turn home to a diesel tune,

My boot presses firmly to the floor,

The diner will have a table for two,

Where tired staff will announce our feast to the cheer of squeaking doors.



# **School** {#school}

Student pass watch rounding glances,

With gazes and doodles that exchange me for a dot,

Shall I make my case again today,

Only to clear the chalk board with my elbow in the air,

And brush my words to dust?

And crossing between desks and chairs,

Between the backpacks and crushed corners of books,

watch the hands through my chalet door,

pinch the pencils by the neck,

To rub the point upon the page.



# **Doodlers**

A crustaceous note floats between the desks,

It opens and time sails out of the bay,

Then flagging on the slack tide,

The note turning student pulls on the rigging,

His brined thoughts evaporate,

And lift in a white trail of curly brackets vanishing into the binding of the bay.

The able students search the fields for shortcuts,

There is a gate, a fence, a stack of stones,

Down their rumen the problem goes,

Bubbling and burping,

And up it pushes, green to the roof of their mouth,

An inch of grass for every acre.



# **Where to go** {#where-to-go}

Shall I be in the foaming waves and brown under toe taking me past the white chew of breakers,

Or with my herd searching for parasols, porcini and walking sticks,

Or under the cleft of a mountain top spinning on my milk stool,

Or in a cave turning tommes and washing them with morge,

Or under the porticos among the beggars and vendors of books,

Or in a churchyard under the grace of pigeons?

Shall I be waiting akimbo for tractors with my stacked wood,

Or cross legged at the stern of a ferry reading the wake like the flapping pages of a book,

Or chasing the herd’s shifts and swing through the woods,

Or resting my phone on my lap in the subway,

To watch my face run the glassy width of Manhattan,

Or shall I be standing under the almond trees between pallets of bees?

Shall I retrace my steps all of my life,

Or fill the potholes of this economy,

Calling the attention of students to the chalkboard?

I know the desire to squeeze for milk one too many,

And to push my hand deeper into the meat grinder.

Seeing the drooping eye and rib cage on the cutting table

And those shackled and hooved pulling on chains between 30 others in Aosta,

I turn back to the road-falling knee sliding alps,

the hey-hoeing dung spreading alps,

The giancianne-drunk glass brimming alps.

Time’s snowflakes fall on my shoulders,

The mountains are dusted like your coat,

Do you hear time’s mallet,

Like snow tapping on my hat,

Watch it vanish at the slightest touch.

The barn is empty and the painted path hairpins left and upward,

The peak is not far off but the cows will be returning shortly,

Shall I go for the path upward?

Can I be wise if I choose to be?

Will I spill the labor of my milk can,

Or ascend like the vapor over the farm.



# **Farewell** {#farewell}

Oh, but look at the hour,

Goodbye, you sweet smelling prince,

I am sorry, I did not see how late it was,

Goodbye, goodbye my art,

I must go and make calculations,

And bill my time for consultations.

Goodbye, old fool,

Goodbye, tender thoughts.

My coals now chalk,

Bones good for broth,

Good bye, you citrus promises,

My mansions of thought,

My alpine ruminations.



# **Onward** {#onward}

Among mountains self-amused in fall reflections,

Rivers splashing their sheet music with cheer of home,

And rain’s feverish typing.

Follow the trail from town,

Beyond the alps you’ll find me,

A blade of green,

Screaming into the world like a babe.
